# Commercial viability and revenue model

## 1. Problem framing

This concept only becomes commercially viable when the value is tied to a specific pain point with clear ROI: delayed payment approvals, high manual rework, stale reference data, or costly cross-border payment coordination. The initial target should not be broad "AI payment optimization" but one measurable workflow for a narrow segment.

## 2. Value drivers

- Lower latency for reference and approval data in the locations where users operate
- Reduced manual handling and fewer exceptions in approval workflows
- Better visibility into decision timing and policy enforcement
- More reliable processing of recurring B2B payment flows across regions
- Less origin load and better predictability in peak periods

## 3. MVP revenue model

The most realistic starting model is a two-part SaaS motion:

- Platform subscription
  - Paid monthly or annual access for a tenant or region
  - Includes core workflow orchestration, cache management, policy controls, and dashboards
- Implementation and integration services
  - Separate fees for customer-specific integration, data mapping, and production rollout support

This approach is easier to sell than taking a share of transaction value in the MVP stage, because it avoids introducing regulatory, compliance, and transaction-processing complexity too early.

## 4. Pricing hypotheses

Use these only as design-partner assumptions to validate:

- Pilot engagement: $10k–$40k for a short design partner period
- Standard tenant plan: $2k–$10k/month depending on volume and support scope
- Enterprise plan: custom annual commitment, premium SLA, and specialized integrations

These figures should be refined using customer interviews, implementation cost estimates, and actual infrastructure usage.

## 5. Commercial readiness checklist

A customer is likely to buy if:

- The workflow is high-volume and measurable
- There is visible manual rework or slow approval time today
- The customer has a regionally distributed operating model
- The integration can sit alongside an existing payment provider and internal systems without major disruption
- Security and approval controls are transparent and auditable

## 6. Pricing strategy

Start with a clear value statement tied to business outcomes, not vague technology benefits. The product should be sold on outcomes such as:

- reduced average workflow time
- reduced rework and duplicate payment handling
- improved serviceability in multi-region operations
- fewer policy exceptions and fewer late approvals

## 7. Risks to avoid

- Charging a fee based on payment value before legal and regulatory review
- Overpromising production blockchain benefits without a validated customer use case
- Building for broad markets before proving the workflow in a narrow, well-defined niche
- Treating AI as a product feature without measurable operational uplift

## 8. Recommendation

For the first commercial phase, position the startup as a workflow acceleration and decision-support layer for B2B payment operations, not as a full payment processor or blockchain platform. That keeps the offering more credible, easier to pilot, and more likely to get paid design partners in a realistic timeframe.
